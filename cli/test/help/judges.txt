$ pinecall judges --help
Usage: pinecall judges [options] [command]

every judge a call meets: Pinecall's, switched on or off; the org's own and the
agent's own; one tried on calls

Options:
  -h, --help             display help for command

Commands:
  list [options]         every judge the list meets: whose, on or off, how it
                         answers, when it runs
  show [options] <name>  one judge whole: whose, its switch, how and when it
                         answers, what it reads, its question
  agreement [options]    how often each judge agreed with the labels people
                         gave, and whether it is trusted
  add [options] <name>   write one of your own; the same name again replaces it
  on [options] <name>    one of Pinecall's judges switched on for the org
                         (--org) or the agent
  off [options] <name>   one of Pinecall's judges switched off for the org
                         (--org) or the agent
  rm [options] <name>    one of your own dropped; Pinecall's are never dropped,
                         only switched off
  try [options] <name>   ask one judge of finished calls and write nothing
  help [command]         display help for command

Every judge is one question a model answers about a finished call, with the whole call in front
of it — the turns, the tool calls, the positions it cites. Three lists, in the order a call meets
them: PINECALL's library (consent, grounded, promises, disclosed, identified, honoured-stop,
ended-well, expected-outcome, relevance, repetition, sentiment; every one off until switched on),
switched on or off for the org (--org) or for one agent, the agent's switch winning; the ORG's
own, asked of every agent's calls; and one AGENT's own, about its job alone.

A judge answers a verdict (held or broken), a score from 1 to 5, or one of its choices; any of
them may answer N/A — the question did not apply, never billed. It runs on every call, only on a
simulated one, or on a trigger: a yes-or-no asked first, and a no is N/A. Each judge that answers
is one eval; on a model of your org's own key (`pinecall judging --model`) evals are not billed.

A judge is only worth its verdicts if it agrees with what a person would have said: dismissing a
case with --judge-was-wrong labels the judge, and `agreement` is how often each judge agreed with
its labels — trusted from 10 compared labels on, agreeing with 80% of them at least.

Examples
  $ pinecall judges add offers-next-slot --asks "The agent offered the next free slot before the caller asked twice."
  offers-next-slot written · 12 judge(s)
  $ pinecall judges off relevance --org
  relevance off · 12 judge(s)
  $ pinecall judges try offers-next-slot --last 3
  CA_8f4a2c  ✓ held  Offered Thursday at 09:30 unasked.  [seq 14]
  3 evals · $0.0031 · nothing was written
  $ pinecall judges agreement
  judge             labelled  compared  agreed  rate  trusted
  offers-next-slot  12        11        8       73%   no
