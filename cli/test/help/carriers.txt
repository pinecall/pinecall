$ pinecall carriers --help
Usage: pinecall carriers [options] [command]

list | show | add | drop the org's carrier accounts: Twilio, a SIP peer,
WhatsApp, a Facebook Page, a mailbox

Options:
  -h, --help      display help for command

Commands:
  list            every carrier account of the org, oldest first
  show [account]  one account, named, never its secret
  add             keep an account: its fields from the flags, its secrets from
                  stdin
  drop [account]  an account let go; its numbers stay routed until dropped
  help [command]  display help for command

An account is where the org's numbers live: a Twilio account, a SIP peer (its own PBX or a
carrier with no API here), a WhatsApp number at Meta, a Facebook Page (its Messenger, and the
Instagram account linked to it), a mailbox. It is the org's, one for both envs, and
`pinecall numbers import` then routes a number it owns to an agent: a Page by its id on
--channel messenger, its Instagram account by that account's id on --channel instagram.

Every secret is read on stdin and never from the command line: typed with nothing echoed on a
terminal, or piped one per line — a SIP peer's password, then its outbound password when
--outbound-username is given. `add` with an account the org already holds replaces its secret.
A peer's networks wait for Pinecall's operator before 5060 opens to them; `show` says where each
one stands. `drop` forgets an account and leaves its numbers routed until each is dropped.

Examples
  $ pinecall carriers add twilio --account-sid AC0123… --user SK4567… --label Clínica
  $ pinecall carriers add messenger --page-id 1472943555898656 --instagram-id 17841400000000001

Usage: pinecall carriers add [options] [command]

keep an account: its fields from the flags, its secrets from stdin

Options:
  -h, --help           display help for command

Commands:
  twilio [options]     a Twilio account: its SID, an API key and its secret on
                       stdin
  sip [options]        a SIP peer: who calls from where, and how calls go out;
                       the password on stdin
  whatsapp [options]   a WhatsApp Business number: its id, then Meta's token and
                       your Meta app's secret on stdin
  messenger [options]  a Facebook Page, for its Messenger and its Instagram
                       account: its id, then a Page token and your Meta app's
                       secret on stdin
  email [options]      a mailbox: IMAP to read it, SMTP to answer from it; its
                       password on stdin, then a relay's
  help [command]       display help for command

$ pinecall carriers add email --help
Usage: pinecall carriers add email [options]

a mailbox: IMAP to read it, SMTP to answer from it; its password on stdin, then
a relay's

Options:
  --address <address>      the mailbox's address
  --imap-host <host>       where it is read, over TLS
  --smtp-host <host>       where its replies leave: its own server, or a relay
                           such as Amazon SES
  --imap-port <port>       993 when left out
  --smtp-port <port>       465 (TLS) when left out; 587 is STARTTLS
  --username <login>       the IMAP login, when it is not the address
  --smtp-username <login>  a relay's own login; its password is asked after the
                           mailbox's
  --name <name>            the name the contact reads beside the address
  --label <name>
  -h, --help               display help for command
